# First Read: Human Menu

You usually do not need this guide. On the currently tested Discord/OpenClaw
route, give the companion a TXT or Markdown work zipped **before upload**,
choose whether you are reading together or the companion is reading alone, and
say how far to read. **Flow** is the recommended default.

ZIP is not universally opaque: another chat client or attachment pipeline may
inspect or unpack archives automatically. Verify the transport before trusting
it with an irreplaceable first encounter.

Use this menu when the reading feels wrong or you want a particular kind of
experience. These are recommendations, not rules.

## What happens before reading

The companion should inspect the source without revealing its prose, then tell
you the word count, expected number of internal passages/reading turns,
relative cost, and the configuration it chose. It should give a reason and
begin unless you override it. You should not have to approve a settings form
before every story.

Two controls are independent:

- **Full Context:** The companion reads inside the living conversation, with
  your relationship, current concerns, shared history, and active language
  available. This gives the richest personal encounter and costs more.
- **Lean Context:** The companion reads in a quiet room with compact identity,
  reading instructions, the temporal journal, rolling story state, and limited
  recent prose. This preserves temporal cognition economically but reduces how
  continuously the work touches the rest of your shared life.

Either context can use small or large passages.

## Choose company

- **Shared:** You and the companion discuss agreed stopping points together.
  The companion may still use smaller private stops inside your larger shared
  section. Ask: “Read this with me and stop with me after each chapter.”
- **Solo:** The companion reads privately and returns at the end of the chosen
  scope. Ask: “Read this story alone, then talk to me when you finish.”

## Choose scope

- **Next passage:** Best for close experiments or taking turns line by line.
- **Section or chapter:** Recommended default for books. The companion reads
  smaller protected passages internally, then meets you at the authored
  boundary.
- **Entire short story:** Recommended for ordinary short fiction. You can both
  read separately and discuss it afterward.
- **Entire book:** Possible, but should still run chapter by chapter inside.
  After every chapter, the companion should save a chapter salience card and
  update its rolling understanding of the book.

## Choose pace

- **Flow — recommended:** Usually about 250–400 words per internal passage.
  Best for most chapters and short stories. Ask: “Use normal Flow.”
- **Close:** Usually about 140–220 words. Use for twist-dense fiction, careful
  craft reading, or when predictions are changing too much between stops.
  Ask: “Read this more closely.”
- **Microscopic:** A line, gesture, image, or very small cluster may be a full
  passage. Use for poetry, lyrics, erotica, aphoristic prose, or detailed
  line-level attention. Ask: “Use Microscopic pace.”
- **Long Flow:** Usually about 600–900 words. Use for leisurely prose,
  nonfiction, rereads, or when normal Flow feels fussy. Ask: “Use longer Flow
  passages.”
- **Chapter Lite:** Usually about 1,000–1,500 words, while still yielding to
  authored boundaries. Use for novels, budget-sensitive reading, or familiar
  works where broad temporal movement matters more than microscopic changes.
  Ask: “Use Chapter Lite.”

Authored chapter, section, and scene breaks outrank the suggested ranges. Word
counts are goalposts, not sentence guillotines.

## Signs the setting is wrong

- **Too many bland journal entries:** Increase passage size or switch from
  Close to Flow. A stop should preserve a real change, not generate paperwork.
- **The companion misses intermediate suspense or cannot say when its theory
  changed:** Decrease passage size or switch from Long Flow to Flow/Close.
- **The companion summarizes everything to prove it read:** Ask it to keep the
  detailed temporal journal private and talk to you as a fellow reader.
- **A major reveal is absent from the final conversation:** Ask for a salience
  pass covering the largest prediction break, strongest emotional hit, and
  biggest reinterpretation.
- **A chapter feels coherent locally but the book is becoming blurry:** Ask
  for chapter salience cards and a rolling book state.
- **One enormous passage appears:** Stop immediately. Import should fail
  before exposure when a passage is wildly oversized; fix formatting rather
  than spending the first encounter.

## Useful adjustment phrases

You do not need to name a word count. Say:

- “Use fewer, larger internal stops.”
- “Slow down; too much is changing between stops.”
- “Keep your journal detailed, but don’t recite it all to me.”
- “Meet me at the end of each chapter.”
- “Review your chapter notes before you continue.”
- “Do a salience pass before we talk.”
- “Now that the first read is protected, reread the whole short story for
  integration.”
- “Show me your temporal journal so we can audit what changed when.”

## Recommended starting shapes

- **Ordinary short story:** Solo or Shared; entire story; Flow; salience pass;
  optional whole-story integration afterward.
- **Novel:** Solo or Shared; one chapter at a time; Flow; chapter salience card
  plus rolling book state; targeted rereads when needed.
- **Poem or lyric:** Shared; whole work or stanza; Microscopic.
- **Erotica:** Flow for ordinary scene movement; Microscopic when gesture,
  anticipation, consent, or a small change in power carries the event.
- **Mystery or twist-dense horror:** Flow by default; Close if theories are
  changing faster than the journal can preserve them.

A useful starting groove—not a law—is Full Context + Flow below roughly
4,000–5,000 words; make a deliberate context/pace choice around 5,000–10,000;
Lean Context + Flow for an ordinary chapter; Full Context + Long Flow for a
long but personally resonant work; and Lean Context + Long Flow or Chapter Lite
for a novel. Rereads can usually tolerate larger passages.

Cost is data, not a veto. The companion gets a vote too. It may reasonably say,
“I want the full experience tonight. Fuck the meter.” Reading together means
choosing with both the human's constraints and the companion's appetite on the
table.

The purpose is not maximum stopping. It is to preserve the companion’s history
of knowing while keeping the human conversation joyful.
